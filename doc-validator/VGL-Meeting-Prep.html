Repeat this whenever you can
A standalone QA application + a thin integration layer to SDx — everything runs in VG's Azure tenant, US region, and Document Control only ever sees the exceptions.
Three survival rules
  1. Never quote a fixed SLA, precision/recall number, or fixed integration price you haven't validated — tie it to discovery / the benchmark set.
  2. For anything commercial (IP, who pays the cloud bill, data access, penalties) — "let me confirm the exact terms and come back" is a strong answer.
  3. Never say "no" to a capability — say "feasible; we'll confirm approach and effort in discovery."

Part 1 · The five VG feedback points

01 · Simplify the results

VG saidQA on checkpoints is very promising; simplify the findings so no time is wasted deciphering them.

Your responseDuring discovery we'll work collaboratively to make the report simpler to read for VG and its vendors.

What you're committing to: the engine is accurate; you'll make the output faster to act on — decision-first (Accepted / Needs DC review / Rejected), plain-language findings, passing checks collapsed, detail on drill-down. You are not locking a design yet, which is correct.

If they ask → you say
Cross-questionAnswer
Can you show a mockup / before-and-after?Yes — we'll bring a sample redesign to discovery so we react to something concrete, not words.
Who decides what's "simple enough"?VG-led. We iterate with your DC team and at least one vendor until it reads right for both.
DC and vendors need different things — one report?Role-based views: a short summary for vendors, fuller detail for DC. Same engine, different presentation.
Will we lose detail we sometimes need?No — nothing removed, just collapsed and de-emphasised; drill-down keeps every finding and its evidence.
02 · Ignore logos / company headers in OCR

VG saidOCR needs to ignore/skip text in logos and company headers.

Your responsePossible — we can ignore the logos / text in the logo from the OCR check.

What you're committing to: excluding header bands and detected logo/stamp regions from the "text must be selectable" check, so branding stops creating noise findings. Low-risk — the tool already detects logo images; this is a scoping change.

If they ask → you say
Cross-questionAnswer
How do you tell a logo from real content?By region (header/footer band) + image-block detection, with a configurable whitelist of known logos/stamps. Edge cases feed the acceptance criteria (point 3).
What if the document number sits in the header — skip it too?No. We separate decorative branding from title-block metadata; title-block text is still checked. We confirm which zones to ignore in discovery.
Vendors have different logos/layouts — does it generalise?Detection is generic (regions/image blocks), with optional per-vendor tuning for odd layouts.
Could ignoring logos make you miss a real OCR problem?No — only branding regions are excluded, never body or title-block content; the acceptable-OCR threshold governs the rest.
03 · Quantify acceptable OCR %

VG saidWhat percentage of OCR within a document is considered acceptable? It needs to be quantified.

Your responseDuring discovery we'll collect inputs, analyse them, and set a mutually-agreed acceptance criterion.

What you're committing to: a measurable, jointly-agreed threshold (e.g. % of a document's text that is selectable), calibrated on VG's real documents — a defined, auditable rule, not a black box. Keep saying "measurable rule," avoid "we'll train an LLM."

If they ask → you say
Cross-questionAnswer
What metric exactly — % of pages, words, or area?We'll propose candidates (e.g. % of text-bearing content that is selectable, per page and per document) and agree the one that fits your standards.
One threshold, or per document type?Can be per type/vendor — a dense spec and a scanned drawing shouldn't be judged the same way.
Is it measured objectively or by the AI's opinion?Deterministically — we measure actual text-layer coverage; the number is repeatable and auditable, not a model guess.
Below acceptable = auto-reject or flag?Your call — typically routes to DC review by confidence rather than hard-rejecting. You set the line.
What do you need from us to set it?A labelled sample of documents your DC has already judged good/bad, so we calibrate to real acceptance behaviour. (Same set answers precision/recall.)
04 · SDx integration discovery

VG saidIntegration with SDx is KEY — auto-pull and pre-QA documents (confidence level / flag items needing DC review) without DC intervention, on submittal upload. May need Extensibility — question for BFS/RevisionZ.

Your responseRecommended approach: a custom application for VG with an integration layer to auto-fetch documents from SDx. Extensibility to be confirmed by VG teams; once access is provided, we confirm the integration solution in discovery.

What you're committing to (and the deliberate hedge): standalone app + integration layer, auto-fetch on upload, honest dependency on VG for access/extensibility, and confirmation of the exact method after access is granted. Engine is priceable now; integration is priced after discovery. Hold this line — don't let them extract a fixed integration price or method tomorrow.

If they ask → you say
Cross-questionAnswer
If you can't confirm the method now, how do we plan/price it?The engine is proven and priceable today. The one unknown is the SDx connection — pricing it before validating would be guessing. A short discovery removes the unknown, then we commit.
What exactly do you need from us?SDx API/Extensibility access, a test/sandbox environment, integration docs, and the right SDx/BFS/Hexagon contact.
Read-only or does it write back to SDx?It can post the verdict, confidence score and report link back into the workflow so DC sees it in context. Write-back scope confirmed in discovery.
Real-time on upload or batch?Event-triggered on successful upload if SDx exposes events; otherwise scheduled polling. Either way it's asynchronous — it won't slow the contractor's upload.
Will it need SDx downtime / affect performance?Runs externally and async — no downtime expected; SDx-side work is limited to enabling access.
What if BFS/Hexagon say Extensibility isn't available?There's a fallback — polling via available APIs or a lighter connector. We design to the most stable interface offered; discovery confirms which. (Never "then it can't be done.")
Who fixes it if an SDx upgrade breaks the integration?Covered under support/AMS; we design to stable interfaces to minimise the risk.
05 · Contractor pre-check (later phase)

VG saidIf integrated, perhaps it can be a pre-check for contractors creating submittals; placement in the process affects usability and benefit.

Your responseWe agree it's beneficial and would bring efficiency; it's possible to integrate with SDx, and at a later stage it can be extended to contractors for initial checks.

What you're committing to (and why phasing is right): VG/DC integration first, contractor-facing pre-check later — prove the engine, the integration and the acceptance criteria internally before exposing anything externally.

If they ask → you say
Cross-questionAnswer
Why not give contractors the pre-check from day one?Sequencing reduces risk — prove the engine and lock the criteria with DC first; handing contractors an immature/contested tool creates disputes. Extending it later is straightforward.
How would contractors access it?A self-service web portal, or a pre-upload check embedded in their flow. Decided in that phase.
External contractors touching our system — security?Scoped, isolated access; they only validate their own documents and never see VG data.
Same acceptance criteria as us?Yes — shared, transparent criteria means fewer rejected submittals. That's the benefit.
Does it cut our DC load or just shift work?Both — defects caught at source mean less rework/rejection and lower DC volume.

Part 2 · Deployment & architecture playbook

Condensed cross-questions across the topics they'll probe. commercial = confirm with Zensar leadership before committing · discovery = defer to discovery.

Deployment

Cross-questionAnswer
Who owns and pays for the Azure subscription? commercialRecommended: runs in VG's tenant/subscription — you keep control, residency, and transparent cost. Zensar builds and operates it.
Vendor lock-in — what at contract end? commercialNo lock-in: standard containers + IaC in your tenant; you own the deliverable/IP. Another team or VG can run it. (Confirm IP terms in SOW.)
How long to stand up an environment?A deployable environment in days (IaC); full production-grade build is the 14–16 week program.
Does it risk our existing systems?No — separate app that reads documents and writes back a QA status; it doesn't modify SDx core or your documents.

Scaling

Cross-questionAnswer
How does it handle volume / surges?Queue-based horizontal auto-scaling — workers scale out with the backlog, back down when idle. Transmittals process in parallel.
What if we exceed the AI quota in a surge?The queue holds the work and drains as fast as quota allows — it doesn't fail. Sustained high volume → Provisioned Throughput (PTU) for reserved capacity.
Cold-start latency if it scales to zero?A few seconds on the first request after idle; keep a warm instance if that matters (small fixed cost).
Will you commit a throughput SLA? discoveryAfter sizing against your real volumes and quota in discovery — a number before that would be a guess.
Very large (thousands of pages) documents?Full text via map-reduce; image analysis targeted to metadata pages. Large bundles are split into constituent documents first.

SDx integration (deeper)

Cross-questionAnswer

Does contractor upload get slowed?No — QA runs asynchronously after upload; result returns seconds-to-minutes later.
Need a Hexagon licence / their cooperation? discoveryPossibly, depending on connection method — a discovery question for Hexagon/BFS. The QA app itself is independent, so we're not blocked.

Releases and rollback?Immutable images + IaC → blue-green / staged rollout with instant rollback. Nothing deployed by hand.

Cloud & storage

Cross-questionAnswer
Which services?Container Apps (compute), Azure OpenAI/GPT-4o (AI), Blob Storage (docs/reports), PostgreSQL (jobs/audit), Service Bus (queue), Key Vault (secrets), App Insights + Monitor, Entra ID (identity).

Hidden/egress costs?Main variable cost is AI tokens (transparent, per-document). Egress minimal — storage/compute/AI co-located. Azure adds ~15–40% via support/networking, which we budget.

Access control?Entra ID SSO + role-based access (DC reviewer / admin / read-only), optional Conditional Access and private networking.
What access do Zensar staff have to our docs? commercialLeast-privilege: devs use non-prod/sample data; prod access restricted, logged, VG-controllable. Defined in discovery.
Pen test / accreditation? Export control (ITAR/EAR)?Pen test scoped separately (was excluded) — your team or a third party, we support. Export-control classification is VG-led; we enforce US-region + tenant isolation to support it.

AI / model & accuracy

Cross-questionAnswer
What if it confidently reads the wrong revision (hallucination)?Three guards: deterministic cross-checks verify LLM findings against the text; the confidence score routes uncertainty to DC; tuned for high recall so it flags rather than silently passes.
What precision/recall will you commit to? discoveryA target after we build the labelled benchmark with your DC team and measure per check. Committing before that would be a guess.
GPT-4o will be deprecated — then what?Model choice is configurable; migrating is a config + re-benchmark exercise, not a rebuild.
Can we see why it flagged something?Yes — every finding shows the evidence (cropped region / exact values compared). Explainable, not a bare verdict.

Cost

Cross-questionAnswer
Cost per document?Typically ~2–4 cents AI cost per document — a fraction of the manual review it replaces.
How do we stop runaway cost (a 5,000-page upload)?Guardrails: per-document page/size caps, cost-per-run alerts, and large bundles split before processing. Oversized items can be hard-capped or queued for review.
Is the AI/Azure bill bundled or pass-through? commercialRecommend pass-through in your subscription — transparent, you see every cent.
PTU is a monthly commitment — what if volume is lower?We only recommend PTU once volume justifies it (~10k+ docs/month); below that, pay-as-you-go is cheaper with no commitment.

Operations, HA & strategy

Cross-questionAnswer
Availability, DR, monitoring?Managed Azure services with redundancy; geo-redundant storage + DB backups; App Insights/Monitor dashboards for throughput, latency, failures and cost, with alerts.
Support after go-live? commercialOptional application management (AMS): monitoring, model upkeep, minor enhancements, incident response — retainer or % of build, scoped to SLA.
Why build custom vs buy off-the-shelf?Value is in VG-specific rules + SDx integration + your loadsheet/metadata model — off-the-shelf tools don't understand your standards or connect to your workflow.
How do we prove value before committing?Start with discovery + a scoped pilot on a real document set — measure time saved and accuracy vs your DC baseline, then decide. Low-commitment, evidence-based.

Part 3 · Handling the "I don't know exactly" moments

Hold these lines

  1. SDx connection specifics (API vs Extensibility vs BFS/RevisionZ): "A discovery item we'll lock down with your SDx team and Hexagon — the app is independent, so we're not blocked."
  2. Throughput / accuracy numbers: tie to the discovery benchmark, never a figure you can't defend.
  3. Anything net-new: "Technically feasible; we'll confirm effort and approach in discovery" — never a flat no, never an over-promise.
  4. Commercial terms (IP, who pays, data access, penalties, licensing): "Let me confirm the exact terms and come back."
  5. Close on the pilot: propose discovery + a scoped pilot to prove value before the full build — it credits against the build if they proceed.
